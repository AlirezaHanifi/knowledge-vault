Replicating a counter

A client sends increment-by(n) to the leader, which replicates the change to two followers over point-to-point links. Synchronous replication answers the client after every follower acknowledges, asynchronous immediately, and quorum after a majority. The change travels either as the new state or as the operation.

Illustrates “Replication lag”, “Synchronous vs. asynchronous replication”, “State-based vs. log-based replication” and the leaderboard aha! moment (pp. 111–115, Figures 8.5–8.8). Message latency (2 ticks per hop), the tick model and the read buttons are the vault’s. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • Inherent lag: even with healthy links, a follower applies each change a few ticks after the leader, because updates can’t reach every replica in the same step.
    • Imposed lag: cut a link and that follower falls further behind. A synchronous client now waits indefinitely; a quorum client still gets its answer from the leader plus the other follower.
    • State vs. log: the wire carries counter = 5 or increment-by(3). Applying a state message twice is harmless; applying an operation twice is not, which is why log-based replication needs each operation exactly once and in order.
    • Reading from a follower can return a value older than one the client already had acknowledged, which is the book’s warning about “followers for reads”.
