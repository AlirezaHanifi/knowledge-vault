Majority-voted redundancy

Redundancy is duplication plus coordination. Three copies of a gate (a, b, c) are coordinated by a majority gate R = (a ∧ b) ∨ (b ∧ c) ∨ (a ∧ c), so one failed copy is outvoted. The majority gate is replicated too, so that it isn’t the new single point of failure.

Illustrates “Redundancy” (pp. 107–108, Figure 8.1). The gate function (AND), the failure modes, the rule that the system output is the majority of the three voters, and the simulation are the vault’s. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • One stuck or flipped gate is outvoted by the other two. Two failed gates with the same wrong output win the vote.
    • A single voter would bring back a single point of failure. With three voters, one failed voter is outvoted as well.
    • Redundancy only pays off when parts are reliable. With p below 0.5, three copies are worse than one, because the majority is then more likely to be wrong.
