Partitioning and balancing

Partitioning assigns items to partitions; balancing assigns partitions to nodes. An assignment strategy is judged by variance (how evenly items spread) and relocation (how many items move when the number of partitions changes). The book’s experiment found range partitioning uneven, hashing even but disruptive, and consistent hashing good at both.

    What to notice

    • Range by first letter follows the language: partitions holding a–e and p–t are hotspots, as in the book’s Listing 7.4.
    • hash mod n is almost perfectly even, but going from 5 to 6 partitions keeps an item in place only when h mod 5 = h mod 6, so about 5 in 6 items move.
    • Consistent hashing moves only the arcs the new partition takes over, roughly 1/6 of the items, while staying nearly even.
    • Overpartitioning lets nodes come and go by moving whole partitions, but once there are as many nodes as partitions, more nodes stay idle.
