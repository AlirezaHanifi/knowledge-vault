Two-Phase Commit: recovery and blocking

Each participant persists a log entry before it acts. After a crash, an RM decides what to do from its last log entry: no promise yet → abort; Vote-to-Commit → ask the coordinator; Commit → redo; Abort → undo. An RM that voted to commit can’t decide alone, so a crashed coordinator can block it.

Illustrates “Two-Phase Commit protocol”, “In the presence of failure” and “Improvement” (pp. 83–88, Figures 6.6–6.12). The scenario list and the coordinator’s recovery time are the vault’s; the recovery rules are the book’s. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • RM crashes before voting. It hasn’t promised anything, so on recovery it logs Abort and tells the coordinator, which aborts everyone. Nothing blocks.
    • RM crashes after voting to commit. It has promised, so on recovery it must ask the coordinator for the decision.
    • Coordinator crashes after telling RM1 to commit (Figure 6.11). RM2 is blocked. Asking RM1 helps here: RM1 committed, so RM2 may too.
    • Coordinator crashes before any Commit (Figure 6.12). Nobody knows the decision. Asking peers doesn’t help, and timing out to abort is unsafe: when the coordinator returns with Commit, an RM whose timer hasn’t fired commits while the other has already aborted.
