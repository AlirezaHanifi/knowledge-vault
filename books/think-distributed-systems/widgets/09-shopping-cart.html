Chapter 9 · Consistency

The eventually consistent shopping cart

During a partition, both cart replicas stay available and accept changes. When the partition heals they must converge, which means resolving the conflict. Amazon’s rule keeps added items over removed ones, so nothing a customer added disappears, but a removed item can come back.

Illustrates “The shopping cart”, “Variants of eventual consistency” and “Implementation” (pp. 124–126, Figure 9.9, Table 9.4). The “Figure 9.9” button replays the book’s example. Last write wins and the observed-remove set are the vault’s additions for comparison. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • Figure 9.9 with adds win: replica 1 removes A and adds B, replica 2 adds C, and the merged cart is {A, B, C}. A comes back.
    • Last write wins keeps whichever replica changed last and silently drops the other side’s changes, here item B or C.
    • A grow-only set converges trivially because its merge is a union, but it can’t express removal at all.
    • An observed-remove set tags each add, and a remove deletes only the tags it has seen. It ends with {B, C}: the removal of A sticks, and concurrent adds survive.
