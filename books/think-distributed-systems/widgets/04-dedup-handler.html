Deduplicating a retried request

The sender retries until it gets a response, so the receiver must expect duplicates. The receiver keeps a durable set of requests it has seen. Where the handler records the request, relative to processing it, decides what a crash in between does: the book’s two failed attempts give at-most-once or at-least-once processing, and only recording and processing in one transaction gives exactly-once processing semantics.

Illustrates “Silence and chatter”, “Exactly-once processing semantics” and “Idempotence” (pp. 60–64, the two failed attempts and Listing 4.3). The crash points, the lost-response option and the single retry are the vault’s. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice
    
  
      
    
    • Track, then process. A crash between the two lines leaves the request marked as received but never processed. The retry is skipped: at most once.
      • 
    
    • Process, then track. A crash between the two lines leaves the transfer done but unmarked. The retry runs it again: at least once.
      • 
    
    • Transactional. The transfer and the record commit together or not at all, so a crash rolls both back and the retry runs exactly one effective transfer.
      • 
    
    • A lost response looks the same to the sender as a crash. Every handler then sees a duplicate, and only the record of the request stops it from transferring twice.
