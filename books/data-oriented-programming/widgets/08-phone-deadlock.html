The phone-call deadlock

All weekend, Theo and Joe call each other at exactly the same times, and the line is always busy. Each holds one resource, his own line, and waits for the other’s. “Mutexes are like phone calls, and atoms are like text messages”: a message never waits for the other side.

Illustrates the busy-line story and Theo’s closing analogy (pp. 164, 174). Modeling each line as a lock, the timing and the retry policy are the vault’s. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • A deadlock needs each side to hold one resource and wait for the other: here, each caller’s own line. Starting a few minutes apart avoids it; starting together repeats it, all weekend.
    • Hanging up and retrying breaks the deadlock only if the two retries drift apart. With identical habits they collide again, which is the book’s busy weekend.
    • Text messages are swaps into an atom: each sender appends to the conversation without holding anything, so nobody ever waits and both messages arrive.
