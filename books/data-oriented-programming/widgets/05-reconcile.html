Reconcile two mutations

Two mutations start from the same previous version. The first to commit simply fast-forwards. The second finds that current has moved: the commit diffs previous→next and previous→current, and if the two diffs touch no common information path it merges; otherwise it aborts.

Illustrates sections 5.2 and 5.4–5.5 (pp. 94–108): the tiny Watchmen library of listing 5.6, the two mutations of listing 5.7, the diff and information paths of listings 5.4–5.12, and reconcile of listing 5.16, with its merge condition corrected as the chapter note explains. The other mutations are the vault’s. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice
    
  
      
    
    • The first commit is always a fast-forward: current is still previous, compared by reference, which is safe and fast with immutable data.
      • 
    
    • The book’s example merges: the publication year and the title and author name live at different paths, so both mutations survive.
      • 
    
    • Two mutations on the same path abort the second one, such as two members borrowing the same copy. DOP doesn’t ask anyone to resolve the conflict; the mutation is simply not committed.
