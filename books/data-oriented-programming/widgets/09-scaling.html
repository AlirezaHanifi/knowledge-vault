Copying at scale

Structural sharing by shallow copying, as in chapter 4, copies a whole map or array at each level: fine for small data, slow for big collections. Persistent data structures copy only a path, so the cost grows with log N and stays “effectively constant” up to the largest collections that fit in memory.

Illustrates sections 9.1–9.2 (pp. 176–183): why shallow copying slows down as collections grow, log₂ and log₃₂ depths, and the 10-billion-element bound (about 33 versus 6.64 levels). The 4-byte elements follow the book; the 64-byte cache line and the cost model are the vault’s. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • A shallow copy grows with N: ten billion elements means copying ten billion slots for every update.
    • Path copying grows with log N: 34 levels at branching factor 2, 7 at 32. The book rounds that to “effectively constant”, though not constant in theory.
    • Cache lines are the vault’s way to show the book’s point: a 32-slot node of 4-byte elements spans two 64-byte lines, so a 32-way update touches a few dozen lines, while a binary tree touches one line per level but many more levels.
