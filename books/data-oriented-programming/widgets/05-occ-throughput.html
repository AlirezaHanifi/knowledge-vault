Optimistic or locked

A global lock lets one mutation compute at a time. Optimistic concurrency lets every mutation compute in parallel and checks for conflicts only at commit, aborting and retrying the losers. It pays off when conflicts are rare, which is the “optimistic” assumption.

Illustrates the strategy of sections 5.1 and 5.5 (pp. 92–94, 108): optimistic concurrency control, aborts on conflict, and its assumption that conflicts are rare.     What to notice

    • With many fields, optimistic wins by about the number of workers: everyone computes at once, and almost every commit succeeds.
    • With very few fields, aborts eat the advantage. Every abort wastes a whole calculation; at a single hot field, optimistic can do no better than one commit per calculation time, like the lock.
    • The lock never aborts but never overlaps, so its throughput is flat: one mutation per calculation time, whatever the data.
