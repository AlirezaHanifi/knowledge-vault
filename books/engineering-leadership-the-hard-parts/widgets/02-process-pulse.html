Process pulse

Most leaders guess at process problems. The book’s Process Pulse asks the people who live with the process instead. Read a simulated team’s answers, pick the single pattern that slows the most people, run one two-week experiment, and rerun the pulse.

Illustrates “Exercise: The Process Pulse Survey”, pp. 29–31. The six questions, the patterns to look for and the four experiments are the book’s; the three teams, their answers and how they respond to an experiment are the vault’s invented model.

    What to notice

    • Read the results for patterns, not complaints: waiting on approvals, inventing workarounds, struggling to find focus time.
    • The open answers often carry the real story — the unspoken rules and backdoor processes behind the numbers.
    • Fix one thing. Trying to fix everything at once is how nothing changes; an experiment aimed at the wrong pattern barely moves the pulse.
    • Rerunning the survey and sharing what you learned is what turns feedback into trust.
