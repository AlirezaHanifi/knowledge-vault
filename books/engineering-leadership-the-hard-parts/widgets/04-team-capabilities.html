Chapter 4 · Building Cohesive Teams

Team capability check

A capability framework does for teams what a ladder does for individuals: it shows what “better” looks like. Rate a team you know on the book’s seven capabilities — each with the book’s own strong and weak example — and the check suggests the one or two to work on first.

Illustrates “Capabilities as a Team-Level Tool” and “What Great Teams Do”, pp. 67–71. The capabilities and examples are the book’s; the three-step rating and the “weakest first” suggestion are the vault’s simplification of its assess → pick a few → grow loop.

    What to notice

    • The examples are observable: you can see a team flag a dependency two weeks out, or learn of an outage from a customer’s post. That keeps the assessment a conversation, not a grade.
    • You don’t need all seven on day one. Assess, pick one or two, build them, then reassess.
    • Several weak examples are really trust problems — going silent for three weeks, blaming another team’s API — so improving one capability often lifts others.
