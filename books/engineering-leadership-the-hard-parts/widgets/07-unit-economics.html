Unit economics

Unit economics turn a technical decision into a business one. Start from the book’s example — $50,000 a month of infrastructure for 1,000 customers is $50 each — then try its two levers: a feature that saves $10,000 a month, and a DevOps hire who cuts infrastructure cost by 25%.

Illustrates “Understanding the income side” and “Cost management”, pp. 125–126. The definitions and example figures are the book’s; the default price ($500 a month) and other direct costs ($100 a customer) are the vault’s, chosen so that the book’s 70% margin and its “$10,000 a month = 2% of margin” both hold.

    What to notice

    • COGS includes every direct cost of serving a customer — infrastructure, third-party fees, support — not just the cloud bill.
    • Cutting infrastructure moves gross margin by its share of revenue: here $10,000 of $500,000 monthly revenue is two points.
    • Lifetime value is where reliability shows up in money: a customer who churns two months early takes two months of revenue with them.
    • The vault’s defaults are illustrative. Plug in your own numbers — the point is to talk about engineering work in these terms.
