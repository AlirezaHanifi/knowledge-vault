Chapter 3 · Navigating Chaos

Pilot, medic or engineer?

Range means switching on purpose. First read the signals — burnout often masquerades as resistance, and the two need opposite responses. Then pick the mode each moment needs: pilot to zoom out, medic to stabilize, engineer to get into the work.

Illustrates “Four Lenses for Reading Context” (p. 39) and “Shift Between Roles” (pp. 51–56). The signals, modes and situations are the book’s, including April’s post-incident meeting (p. 56); the sorting exercise is the vault’s. Implementation is the vault’s own.

Event log

    What to notice

    • Resistance is loud — pushback and vocal concerns. Burnout is quiet — withdrawal, slipping quality, compliance with nothing behind it.
    • Treating burnout as resistance (selling the vision harder) piles on more load; the book’s answer is to protect capacity: slow down, cut scope, allow recovery.
    • One meeting can need all three modes. April moves from pilot to medic to engineer as the conversation changes — and the team needs to know why you switched.
    • Each mode backfires when overused: a pilot feels distant to an overwhelmed team, an engineer undermines autonomy, and medic work can become unequal emotional labour.
