Watermarks and late data

A streaming job can't wait forever for stragglers. It tracks the maximum event time it has seen and subtracts an allowed lateness to get the watermark. Events older than the watermark are late and are ignored, and a window is emitted once the watermark passes its end. The watermark never moves back.

Illustrates “Pattern: Late Data Detector” (pp. 51–57). It replays Table 3-1 (two micro-batches, 30-minute lateness, p. 54) and Table 3-2 (five events of 2023-06-30, 1-hour lateness, 10-minute windows, p. 56). Each step processes one micro-batch: events are checked against the watermark from before the batch, and the watermark is then updated. Windows on the Table 3-1 stream are the vault's addition. Like the book's table, it ignores Spark's rounding of the watermark to window bounds. Implementation is the vault's own.

    What to notice

    • Lateness is relative to the watermark, not the clock. In Table 3-1, 9:20 and 9:31 are dropped because the first batch already pushed the watermark to 9:36.
    • The watermark only moves forward. A batch whose MAX event time is low produces a smaller candidate, which is discarded (p. 54).
    • Windows wait for the watermark. In Table 3-2 nothing is emitted until 04:31 moves the watermark to 03:31. Then both 03:00 windows close at once.
    • Raise the allowed lateness to 2 hours. 01:50 is now accepted, but every window is held in state longer.
