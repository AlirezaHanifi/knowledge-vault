The last slice of pie

Two waiters each see one slice in the display case and promise it to their customers. The problem isn’t that both can change the count. It’s that neither can be sure its view is still true: checking and taking aren’t atomic. Shared state is incorrect state.

Illustrates Topic 34, Shared State Is Incorrect State (pp. 299–306, Tip 57): the waiters’ code, the semaphore (the diner’s plastic leprechaun), get_pie_if_available, and the forgotten unlock when update_sales_data raises.     What to notice

    • With no lock, almost every interleaving (240 of 246 with one slice) lets both waiters read “1 slice” before either takes it, so two customers are promised one pie. Only when one waiter finishes taking before the other looks is it safe.
    • The semaphore serializes the check and the take. It works only because both waiters follow the convention of holding the leprechaun first.
    • Forget the ensure and let update_sales_data raise: waiter A never unlocks, and waiter B waits forever. That is the case the book fixes with ensure or protect.
    • get_pie_if_available moves the check and take into one call on the resource, so callers can’t get it wrong.
