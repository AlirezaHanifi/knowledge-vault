The blender contract

Exercise 14’s blender has ten speeds (0 is off). It can’t run empty, and its speed changes one step at a time. A contract makes those rules explicit. A failed precondition is the caller’s bug; a failed postcondition or invariant is the blender’s.

Illustrates Topic 23, Design by Contract, Exercise 14 (pp. 191–202). The book asks the reader to write the contract; the one shown here (including “don’t empty while running”) is the vault’s answer, not the book’s appendix. The faulty-motor option is the vault’s. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • Preconditions protect the blender from its callers. Jumping from 0 to 2, or running it empty, is rejected at the call and blamed on the caller, where the bug is.
    • With checking off, the same calls go through. The blender ends up running empty, a state its invariant says can never happen, and nothing says whose fault it was.
    • A faulty motor breaks the postcondition getSpeed() == x even for a correct call. That bug belongs to the blender: the supplier broke its promise.
