Outrunning your headlights

Low beams light about 160 feet, but at 40 mph a car needs 189 feet to stop, and 464 feet at 70 mph. In software the headlights are feedback. The rate of feedback is your speed limit, so take small steps, always.

Illustrates Topic 27, Don’t Outrun Your Headlights (pp. 222–225, Tip 42): the NHTSA figures of 160 ft throw, 1.5 s reaction time and 17.02 ft/s² deceleration. The stopping-distance formula (reaction distance plus v²/2a) reproduces the book’s 189 ft and 464 ft. The project model in the lower half (random changes of direction, a fixed cost per feedback check) is the vault’s. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • The book’s numbers check out: 1.5 s of reaction plus braking at 17.02 ft/s² gives 189 ft at 40 mph and 464 ft at 70 mph. Above about 36 mph you are driving faster than your headlights can see.
    • Big steps waste work: when the direction changes in the middle of a large committed batch, everything built past that point is thrown away.
    • Tiny steps aren’t free in this model, because each feedback check costs something. The cheaper feedback is (a REPL, a unit test), the smaller the best step becomes.
