Broken windows

One unrepaired broken window signals that nobody cares, and decay speeds up from there. The book’s advice: don’t live with broken windows. Fix them as soon as you find them, or at least board them up so the damage stops spreading.

Illustrates Topic 3, Software Entropy (pp. 42–45, Tip 5). The model is the vault’s: each edit has a 2% base chance of leaving a mess, plus the share of modules showing visible (unboarded) mess; the team makes ten edits a week. Edits in messy modules deliver half a feature. Fixing a window costs one edit; boarding costs a fifth of one. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • Ignoring starts slowly, then accelerates: each new mess raises the chance of the next. This is the feedback loop the book describes, where decline speeds up once nobody seems to care.
    • Fixing at once costs a little feature work every week, but keeps the codebase near pristine, so features keep flowing at full speed.
    • Boarding up is cheap and stops the spread, but the boarded modules are still debt. In this model they still deliver half speed until someone really fixes them.
