Iterating the schedule

Often the only way to know a project’s timetable is to gain experience on that same project. Work in thin slices, then refine the estimate of the remaining iterations after each one. Iterate the schedule with the code.

Illustrates “Estimating Project Schedules” in Topic 15, Estimating (pp. 138–140, Tip 24). The project, the team’s pace (random around 8 points per iteration) and the scope found along the way are the vault’s model. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • The up-front estimate is a single guess that can’t improve: whatever it misses (true size, pace, scope found along the way) shows up only at the end.
    • The iterated forecast uses the pace the team has actually shown on this project, so after a few iterations it closes in on the real finish.
    • Scope found along the way moves the target. The forecast follows it, which is the book’s reason for saying “I’ll get back to you” rather than committing at the coffee machine.
