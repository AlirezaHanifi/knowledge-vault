The piña colada scheduler

An activity diagram shows which actions could happen at once. A bartender who makes a piña colada one step at a time would lose their job. Tasks 1, 2, 4, 10 and 11 can all start up front, and while the blender runs for a minute the bartender’s hands are free.

Illustrates Topic 33, Breaking Temporal Coupling (pp. 292–297, Tip 56): the twelve steps and the one-minute liquefy are the book’s (pp. 293–295). The other durations, the dependency arrows (the vault’s reading of the book’s diagram) and the greedy scheduler are the vault’s. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • Serially, the drink takes the sum of all the steps. Following the dependencies, a second hand already shortens it, because the openings, measuring and fetching don’t depend on each other.
    • Five hands are what it takes to start every initial task at once (p. 296). Beyond the hands you need, the one-minute liquefy is the floor: it’s on every path to serving.
    • Liquefying takes time, but not the bartender’s time. Untick “watch the blender” and glasses and umbrellas get fetched during the minute: the book’s “take time, but not time in your code”.
