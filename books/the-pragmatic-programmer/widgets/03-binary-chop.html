Binary chop

When a bug appears somewhere in a long history or a big dataset, don’t step through it one item at a time. Chop it in half, test, and keep the half that still shows the problem: with n items that takes about log₂ n tests instead of up to n.

Illustrates “The Binary Chop” in Topic 20, Debugging (pp. 174–175): finding the first release with a regression, and finding the record that crashes a program. The history size, the hidden culprit (seeded) and the visual are the vault’s. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • Stepping back one release at a time costs as many tests as there are releases since the bug, which can be all of them.
    • Chopping needs about log₂ n tests: 7 for 128 releases, 10 for 1,024. Every test halves what’s left, whether the item is a release or a record.
    • The invariant is what makes it work: the newest release (or the full dataset) is known bad and the oldest is known good, so the culprit is always between them.
