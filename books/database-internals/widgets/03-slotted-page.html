Slotted page: sorted pointers, unsorted cells

A slotted page grows a cell-pointer array from the start of the page and cells from the end. Keys stay in order by reordering the small fixed-size pointers, never the cells. A deleted cell becomes a free segment on an availability list, and later inserts reuse it by first fit or best fit. When no single gap is large enough but the free bytes add up, the page is defragmented.

    What to notice

    • Pointers move, cells don’t. After each insert the pointer array is re-sorted by key, but the cell bytes stay at their offsets; external references use the slot, not the offset.
    • Deletes leave holes. The cell is only marked free and added to the availability list; nothing is shifted.
    • First fit vs best fit. First fit takes the first hole that is large enough and can leave a sliver too small to use; best fit picks the hole that leaves the least.
    • Defragmentation is the fallback. When the next record doesn’t fit anywhere but the total free bytes would hold it, live cells are rewritten contiguously and the holes disappear.
