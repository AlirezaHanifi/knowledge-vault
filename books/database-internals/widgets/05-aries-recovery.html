ARIES: analysis, redo, undo

ARIES is steal/no-force: uncommitted changes may reach disk, and committed ones may not have. After a crash it analyses the log from the last checkpoint to rebuild the transaction and dirty-page tables, redoes every logged change whose page on disk is older, repeating history, and then undoes the transactions that never committed. Each undo writes a compensation log record (CLR), so a crash during recovery never undoes the same change twice.

Illustrates “Recovery”, “Steal and Force Policies” and “ARIES” (pp. 88–93). The workload (three transactions, four pages, one checkpoint and two page flushes) is the vault’s; so are the simplifications: the whole log is durable, checkpoints are a single record, and pages hold one number, and no two uncommitted transactions write the same page (as strict locking would ensure). Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • Redo repeats history. Redo reapplies every logged change, including those of transactions that will be undone, whenever the page on disk is older than the record (its page LSN is lower).
    • Steal means undo. The flush of P4 writes T3’s uncommitted value to disk. Crash before T3 commits and undo must restore the old value.
    • No-force means redo. T1 commits, but its change to P3 was never flushed; only the log makes it durable. Crash at the end and T2, which never committed, is undone on all three pages it touched.
    • CLRs are redo-only. Crash again during undo: the next recovery redoes the CLRs and continues from their undo-next pointer instead of undoing anything twice.
