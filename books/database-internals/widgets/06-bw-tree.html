Bw-Tree: delta chains and compare-and-swap

A Bw-Tree never updates a node in place. Each change is a delta record prepended to the node’s chain. Nodes are found by logical ID through an in-memory mapping table, and a writer installs its delta with a single compare-and-swap on that table entry. If another writer got there first, the CAS fails and it retries. Readers walk the chain from the newest delta down to the base node, so long chains are consolidated into a new base.

Illustrates “Update Chains”, “Taming Concurrency with Compare-and-Swap” and “Consolidation and Garbage Collection” (pp. 120–124, Figure 6-7). The pages, keys, addresses and default threshold are the vault’s. SMOs (split and merge deltas) and epoch-based reclamation are left out. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • Writes never block. A writer reads the page’s current address, builds a delta pointing at it, and swaps the mapping-table entry only if it still holds that address.
    • A lost race costs a retry, not a wait. In the race, writer B’s CAS fails because A changed the address first; B rebuilds its delta on top of A’s and succeeds.
    • Reads pay for chain length. A read visits every delta until it finds the key, then the base; the cost grows until consolidation resets it.
    • Consolidation is just another CAS. The merged base node is written to a new address and installed in the mapping table; the old chain is garbage once no reader holds it.
