Chapter 10 · Leader Election

Bully election, failover lists and split brain

In the bully algorithm the highest-ranked live process becomes leader. A process that notices the leader is gone sends Election to every higher-ranked process. If any answer Alive, it hands over to the highest of them, which announces itself to all lower-ranked processes with Elected. Next-in-line failover skips the round: the old leader published a list of alternatives, and the first live one takes over. Without a majority requirement, a partition lets each side elect its own leader.

    What to notice

    • Who notices matters. If process 1 notices, it sends Election to five processes; if 5 notices, it simply announces itself. The message count changes accordingly.
    • Next-in-line failover replaces a full round with one request to the first alternative on the old leader’s list, and falls back to the next alternative if that one is down.
    • Partition the cluster. Process 6 is alive but unreachable from {1, 2, 3}; that side elects 3 while 6 keeps leading {4, 5, 6}: a split brain.
    • No majority, no safety. None of these algorithms checks that the winner can reach most of the cluster, which is what consensus protocols add.
