Two-phase vs three-phase commit under failures

In two-phase commit the coordinator collects votes and then broadcasts the decision. A cohort that voted yes has promised to wait, so if the coordinator dies before telling it the outcome, the cohort is blocked. Three-phase commit adds a prepare round and timeouts on both sides: a cohort that times out before reaching the prepared state aborts, and one that times out after it commits. That removes blocking, but only if the network never partitions.

Illustrates “Two-Phase Commit”, “Cohort Failures in 2PC”, “Coordinator Failures in 2PC”, “Three-Phase Commit” and “Coordinator Failures in 3PC” (pp. 259–266, Figures 13-1 to 13-6). The scenarios, the three cohorts and the step granularity are the vault’s. Every cohort votes yes, so failures alone decide the outcome. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • 2PC: crash after collecting votes. Every cohort voted yes and now waits; none can decide alone, because the coordinator might have decided either way. Only its recovery, or a replacement collecting votes again, unblocks them.
    • 2PC: crash mid-broadcast. One cohort learned “commit” before the crash. Because the decision is unanimous, the others can safely copy it from that peer.
    • 3PC: the same crashes don’t block. Cohorts that never reached prepare abort on timeout; cohorts that were all prepared commit on timeout. Everyone ends in the same state.
    • 3PC: a partition during prepare. The prepared side commits on timeout while the cut-off cohort aborts on timeout. Everyone followed the protocol, and the transaction is both committed and aborted.
