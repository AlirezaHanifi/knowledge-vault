φ-accrual vs a fixed deadline

A deadline detector suspects a node after a fixed silence. The φ-accrual detector keeps a sliding window of recent heartbeat inter-arrival times, estimates their mean and variance, and turns the time since the last heartbeat into a suspicion level φ: how likely we are to be wrong if we declare the node dead now. A node is suspected when φ crosses a threshold, so the detector adapts to how noisy the network is.

Illustrates “Phi-Accrual Failure Detector” (pp. 199–200) and the deadline detector (p. 197). The normal-distribution model of inter-arrival times is the book’s; the formula used, φ = −log₁₀(1 − F(Δt)), follows Hayashibara et al. The 1-second heartbeat interval, the 100-sample window, the pauses and the defaults are the vault’s (seeded). Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • Raise the jitter. With noisy heartbeats the deadline detector starts raising false suspicions, while φ’s threshold stretches with the larger variance.
    • A GC pause is a live node going silent: both detectors may suspect it, and both recover when heartbeats resume.
    • Kill the node and compare detection times: a low φ threshold or a short deadline detects faster but suspects more live nodes.
    • φ is a scale, not a verdict. φ = 1 means about a 10% chance of being wrong, φ = 2 about 1%, φ = 3 about 0.1%; applications choose their own threshold.
