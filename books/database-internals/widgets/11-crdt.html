CRDTs: counters and sets that always merge

A conflict-free replicated data type lets replicas accept updates independently, even during a partition, and still converge. Their merge is commutative, associative and idempotent. A G-Counter keeps one slot per replica, merges by element-wise maximum and reads as the sum. A PN-Counter pairs two G-Counters, one for increments and one for decrements. A two-phase set keeps an add-set and a remove-set, and a removed element can never come back.

Illustrates “Strong Eventual Consistency” and “Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types” (pp. 238–240), including the G-Counter example of Table 11-1. The replicas, elements and partition are the vault’s. Merges are state-based (whole states exchanged), the form the book’s counter examples use. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • No update is lost. Increments made on both sides of the partition all survive the merge, because each replica only ever writes its own slot.
    • Order doesn’t matter. States are exchanged in a random order each time, and the result is always the same: max and union are commutative and idempotent.
    • A G-Counter can’t go down. Decrements only change the PN-Counter, whose value is the sum of P minus the sum of N.
    • Two-phase sets remember removals. Remove an element on one side and add it again on the other: after the merge it stays removed, because the remove-set wins.
