Levels and salary bands

Each level of a career ladder has a salary band. Many early levels with narrow bands let you promote people every year and keep pay at a level close together; few levels with wide, overlapping bands make levels clearly distinct and let strong people be paid well without a promotion.

Illustrates “Writing a Career Ladder” (pp. 205–207). The “few levels” bands start from the book’s example — software engineer $50–100K, senior $80–150K (p. 206); the other levels and the whole “many levels” ladder are invented. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • With wide overlapping bands, a strong engineer can earn more than a new senior engineer without being promoted — the book’s example, $50–100K against $80–150K.
    • With narrow bands, people at a level are paid alike, which helps avoid bias, but adjacent levels are hard to tell apart.
    • The breakpoint level (often senior engineer) is where people can stay; expect them to cluster there, and keep the keystone promotions above it rare and celebrated.
