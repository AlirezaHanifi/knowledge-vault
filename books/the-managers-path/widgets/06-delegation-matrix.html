Chapter 6 · Managing Multiple Teams

Delegate or do it yourself?

Two small tables carry much of this chapter. Table 6-1 separates importance from urgency; Table 6-2 decides what to delegate by how complex and how frequent a task is. Sort the book’s own examples and check them against its answers.

Illustrates “Managing Your Time: What’s Important, Anyway?” (Table 6-1, pp. 103–106) and “Decisions and Delegation” (Table 6-2, pp. 108–109). The tasks are the book’s examples; the sorting exercise is the vault’s. Implementation is the vault’s own.

Event log

    What to notice

    • Important but not urgent is the quadrant that slips: preparing for meetings, hiring plans, a conflict with another team’s manager. At this level you’re trusted to handle it before it becomes urgent for your boss.
    • Simple and infrequent work is yours — explaining it would take longer than doing it, even if it feels beneath you.
    • Complex, frequent work is the biggest chance to grow your team: teach the tasks only you know how to do, or you’ll be hard to promote.
