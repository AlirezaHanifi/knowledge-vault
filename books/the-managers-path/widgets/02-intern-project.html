Sizing an intern project

Have a specific, relevant but not urgent project ready, sized so that a new hire could finish it in about half the internship — five weeks of work for a ten-week intern — and plan for the intern to present it at the end.

Illustrates “Mentoring an Intern” and Ask the CTO: Mentoring a Summer Intern (pp. 12–16). The book gives the reasons — interns move slowly, and training and social events take time (p. 16); the numbers for pace, other activities and the week kept for the presentation are the vault’s assumptions.     What to notice

    • Half the internship is not slack: the book expects an intern to move slowly, and training, social events and the final presentation take real weeks (p. 16).
    • A project sized at the whole internship almost never finishes, leaving the intern without a demo — and interns who feel their work mattered come back.
    • Break the project into milestones (p. 13): if the intern races ahead, the next milestone is ready; if they struggle, an early one is still a result.
