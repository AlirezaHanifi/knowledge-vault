A credit-score feedback loop

A good worker hits a misfortune outside their control, misses payments, and their credit score drops. If employers screen applicants by credit score, the drop makes a new job harder to find — and joblessness pushes the score lower still. Compare the same population with and without that screening.

Illustrates “Feedback Loops” (pp. 588–589) and “Responsibility and Accountability” (pp. 587–588). Everything numeric here — 300 workers, monthly steps, shock rate, score changes, hiring odds — is the vault’s own toy model, not data from the book. Implementation is the vault’s own.

What to notice

  • Same people, same luck. Both runs use identical misfortunes; only the screening differs — yet long-term exclusion appears only with it.
  • A spiral, not a verdict. Trapped workers weren’t worse workers; their score fell because they lost a job, and then kept them from the next one.
  • Systems thinking. Looking only at the scoring model, each rejection seems “prudent”; the harm shows up only when you model the people responding to it (p. 589).
  • Tune the screen. A stricter threshold or harsher score drops deepen the trap; recovery while unemployed is impossible in this model, as in the book’s story.
