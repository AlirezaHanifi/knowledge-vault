B-tree pages, lookups and splits

A B-tree breaks the database into fixed-size pages and overwrites them in place. A lookup follows page references from the root down to a leaf; an insert into a full page splits it in two and updates the parent — sometimes all the way up, which is how the tree grows a new root.

Illustrates “B-Trees” and “Making B-trees reliable” (pp. 125–128, Figures 4-5 and 4-6). Pages here hold at most 4 keys, so the branching factor is 5 instead of the several hundred of a real database; leaves hold keys only, and deletes are left out. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • Lookups read one page per level. The depth grows only when the root splits, so it stays logarithmic in the number of keys — the book’s four levels of 4 KiB pages with branching factor 500 hold up to 250 TB.
    • Most inserts write one page. A split writes the two halves plus the parent; occasionally a cascade rewrites a page on every level.
    • A split overwrites several pages. Crash halfway without a WAL: the left half is on disk but the parent never learned about the right half, so its keys are orphaned. With the WAL, recovery replays the insert.
    • Contrast with the LSM-tree. Here pages are modified in place; an LSM-tree only appends and later deletes whole files.
