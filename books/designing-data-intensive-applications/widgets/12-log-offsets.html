Partitioned logs and consumer offsets

A log-based broker appends each message to one partition of a topic, chosen by its key, and gives it an increasing offset. Consumers in a group each own whole partitions and read them in order; the broker only needs to remember each partition’s committed offset — which is exactly why a crash causes re-processing, and a slow consumer can fall off the end of retention.

Illustrates “Using logs for message storage”, “Logs compared to traditional messaging”, “Consumer offsets” and “Disk space usage” (pp. 496–499, Figure 12-3). Three partitions, keys hashed to partitions, offsets committed every few messages, and retention counted in messages rather than disk segments. Implementation is the vault’s own.

    What to notice

    • Order per partition, not per topic. All of one user’s messages go to the same partition, so they stay in order; there is no ordering across partitions.
    • Crash → duplicates. Kill a consumer: its partitions move to a survivor, which resumes from the last committed offset, so everything after it is processed again. Commit more often and fewer repeat — at more bookkeeping cost.
    • At most one consumer per partition. A third consumer helps; a fourth would sit idle with three partitions.
    • The log is a bounded buffer. Pause a consumer: when retention deletes old messages, a consumer that fell too far behind skips them for good.
