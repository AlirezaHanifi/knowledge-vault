Percentiles and tail latency

Response time is a distribution, not a number. The average hides the slow requests that users actually notice, and when one user request fans out to many backend calls, a rare slow call becomes a common slow page.

2 · Fan-out amplifies the tail

A page that needs k backend calls in parallel is as slow as its slowest call. If each call is slow with probability p, the page is slow with probability 1 − (1 − p)k.

What to notice

  • Raise the slow-event probability: the mean moves a little, the median barely moves, but p99 jumps — the percentile tells you what your unluckiest users experience.
  • With 1% slow backend calls and 100 calls per page, well over half of page loads are slow. Backend p99 becomes roughly user-facing p50.
  • This is why high percentiles of backend services matter so much, and why averaging percentiles across machines is meaningless — you need to merge histograms.
