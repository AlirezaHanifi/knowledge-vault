Chapter 10 · Consistency and Consensus

Consensus: elect a leader, replicate a log

Consensus algorithms are single-leader replication with automatic, safe failover. A node that stops hearing from the leader starts an election for a new, higher epoch (Raft calls it a term); a quorum must vote for it, and a quorum must store every log entry before it counts as committed.

Illustrates “From single-leader replication to consensus” and “Subtleties of consensus” (pp. 434–436) and the cost of quorums in “Pros and cons of consensus” (p. 436). A teaching model loosely following Raft: leaders send their whole log rather than incremental entries, there is no log compaction or membership change, and timing is in abstract ticks. Implementation is the vault’s own — not a production algorithm.

    What to notice

    • Epochs make leaders unique per term, not globally. After a partition heals, an old leader with a lower term steps down the moment it sees a higher term.
    • Writes need a majority. Partition the leader into the minority {A,B}: it keeps accepting client writes but can never commit them. The majority side elects a new leader and continues; when the partition heals, the minority's uncommitted entries are overwritten.
    • Votes check log freshness. A node only votes for a candidate whose log is at least as up-to-date as its own, so a new leader always has every committed entry.
    • Five nodes tolerate two failures. Crash a third node and the cluster stops making progress — safe, but unavailable.
