Normalize or denormalize?

A profile says where a person lives. You can store the region once and give every profile its ID (normalized), or copy the region’s name into every profile (denormalized). One makes reads do a join; the other makes a rename touch many rows — and risks copies that disagree.

Illustrates “Normalization, Denormalization, and Joins” (pp. 72–75), which uses the region “Washington, DC, United States” as its example. The profiles, the second region and the cost model (one row read or written = one unit) are the vault’s own simplifications.

    Which wins for a workload?

    What to notice

    • Normalized: one fact, one place. A rename is a single write, and every profile sees it at once — but each profile read needs a second lookup (a join).
    • Denormalized: reads are self-contained, but a rename must update every copy. Step through it: until the last row is written, readers can see two spellings of the same region.
    • The right choice depends on the workload. Frequent reads and rare changes favour copying; frequent changes favour referencing. Many systems keep normalized data as the system of record and maintain denormalized views derived from it.
