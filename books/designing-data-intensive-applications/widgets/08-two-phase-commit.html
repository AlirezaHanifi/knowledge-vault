Two-phase commit and the in-doubt participant

2PC makes a transaction atomic across nodes with two promises: a participant that votes yes gives up the right to abort, and the coordinator’s decision, once written to its log, is final. The price: if the coordinator crashes after the votes, participants can only wait — in doubt, holding their locks.

Illustrates “Two-Phase Commit (2PC)”, “A system of promises” and “Coordinator failure” (pp. 324–328, Figures 8-13 and 8-14). Two participants, one transaction, messages in order; real coordinators also time out and retry. Implementation is the vault’s own.

Scenario

Event log

    What to notice

    • Before the votes, anyone can abort. A no vote or a timed-out prepare makes the coordinator abort everywhere.
    • The commit point is a disk write. The decision is final once it is in the coordinator’s log; after that, commit requests are retried until they succeed — even to a participant that crashed and came back.
    • In doubt. Crash the coordinator after deciding: DB 1 voted yes, so it can neither commit nor abort on its own, and its locks stay held until the coordinator recovers.
    • A timeout doesn’t help. Let DB 1 give up and abort while DB 2 has committed: atomicity is broken. That is why 2PC is called blocking.
