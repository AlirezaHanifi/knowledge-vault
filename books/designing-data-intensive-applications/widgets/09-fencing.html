Leases, process pauses, and fencing tokens

Client 1 holds a lease that lets it write a file. It checks the lease, then stalls in a long garbage-collection pause. The lease expires, Client 2 takes over and writes — and then Client 1 wakes up, still believing it holds the lease, and writes too.

    What to notice

    • The check and the write are not atomic. Client 1 checked its lease at tick 2 and found it valid; nothing stops time passing before it acts on that knowledge.
    • A paused process doesn’t know it was paused. From Client 1’s point of view, no time passed at all.
    • Short pauses are harmless. Drag the pause below the lease length: Client 1 finishes and releases the lease before anyone else needs it.
    • Fencing moves the check to the resource. The storage remembers the highest token it has seen and rejects older ones — the client’s belief no longer matters.
    • Fencing isn’t a cure-all. With a pause of 8–9 ticks, Client 1 writes after its lease expired but before Client 2’s first write; the storage has seen no higher token yet, so it accepts the write.
